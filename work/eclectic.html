Eclectic

Brand Design
2018

A brand identity for a monthly event series built around experimentation and the overlap between creative disciplines. The system needed to flex from one event to the next without losing its center.

An identity that crosses disciplines

Eclectic is a monthly event series built around experimentation. Each gathering pairs creators from different disciplines and lets them work out loud in front of an audience. Past events have brought a chef into conversation with a painter, a fashion designer with a comic-book artist, and a rapper with a chef, but the point is the overlap itself, not any one pairing. The brand had to feel as open-ended as the program.

I built the system around a fixed identity (wordmark, palette, blob shapes) paired with event-specific objects that hint at what each gathering will explore. The system below is the same on every surface, from a poster to a tote bag. Only the surface changes.

A visual language built around overlap

The core shapes are blobs that overlap, merge, and refuse to settle into a single outline. That behavior is the brand idea: disciplines are not separate boxes, they bleed into each other. The palette is three primary colors (red, blue, yellow) picked to feel bold and unfussy. A serif wordmark anchors the playful shapes with something that reads as confident and considered.

Bringing the system online

The same blob system carries through to the web. Shapes shift and reflow as the user moves through the site, so the identity stays alive across every page.

Event invitations as objects, not cards

Each event also gets a physical invitation tied to its topic. The invitation arrives as the object itself, not as a card describing the object. The object is the first hint of what the night will be about.

A welcome kit that brings attendees in

When attendees arrive, they receive a printed kit. The kit orients newcomers to what Eclectic is, tells them what is coming up next, and asks them to make something they can wear for the night.

The third piece in the kit is the name tag. Instead of pre-printed tags, attendees pick a sticker sheet color, fill in their name and discipline, and cut the blob to fit their style. After the event, they are encouraged to add their tag to the Blob Board, a wall installation that becomes a visual record of the disciplines and diversity in the room. The brand stops being something the team prints and becomes something the audience makes.

Carrying the system across staff and swag

The same three colors and the same blob shapes carry across staff polos and attendee tote bags. The point is consistency: the identity does not change between an envelope, a wall, and a piece of clothing. Only the surface does.
