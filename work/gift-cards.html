Etsy: Gift Cards Revamp

Product Design
2024

Etsy's gift cards were 0.2% of GMS. Competitor marketplaces sat between 2% and 3%. I led design across the program revamp, from end-state vision to MVP launch through a TDS partnership. In the 15 months after launch, gift card orders rose +23% and value purchased rose +30%.

Etsy was known for gifting. Gift cards were not.

Today, 25% of Etsy's GMS is gifts. The brand is built around finding the perfect, made-just-for-you gift. But gift cards were only 0.2% of GMS, while competitor marketplaces sat between 2% and 3%.

The opportunity was to build a gift card program that matched Etsy's gifting brand. A bigger program would mean more new buyers, deeper relationships with existing buyers, and a real channel into physical retail.

Vision first, roadmap second

I set the end-state vision first: a gift card program that cements Etsy as the go-to for gifts. The vision deck mapped the full experience from discovery to redemption, including custom card designs, video and text messages, and pairing gift cards with item suggestions.

The vision became the north star. The team worked backward to decide what to ship in 2024 and what to defer. That order kept the launch plan honest about which decisions moved toward the vision and which were tradeoffs.

The strategic call: partner with TDS

To launch at scale in one year, Etsy partnered with TDS as program manager. TDS handled physical card fulfillment, third-party retailer relationships, fraud, and merchant of record for first-party purchases.

The hardest part was the redirect. Buyers click to buy on Etsy, then complete the purchase on a TDS-hosted site. We gave up UX control to gain speed to market, 20% higher auth rates, and access to physical retail and third-party digital channels.

On Etsy, I designed the entry points: the gift card landing page, ingress banners, search suggestions, a Buy It Now button on web, Apple Pay support, custom amount entry, and a refresh of theme designs.

On TDS, I art-directed their team through rounds of feedback to keep the hosted page as close to Etsy's design as possible. The brand and trust had to carry through the handoff so the buyer would not feel they had left Etsy.

Bringing redemption native

Before this work, gift card redemption in the Etsy app opened a web-view. It felt clunky and disconnected. After redeeming, buyers landed on the account page instead of being guided into a shopping mission. The experience missed the moment when buyers were most ready to spend.

I designed a fully native redemption experience. The credit lands in the buyer's balance automatically, the flow stays inside the app, and the buyer is guided straight from redemption into a shopping mission.

Outcome

In the 15 months after launch, gift card orders rose +23% and value purchased rose +30%, mostly from the new physical and third-party digital channels.

Redemption took off. Gift card redemption GMS hit +42% YoY by mid-2025 and crossed 100% in Q4. The native redemption I designed replaced a clunky web-view, removing the friction that kept buyers from continuing into a shopping mission.

Gift card redeemers visited Etsy +4.8% more, purchased +9.3% more, and drove +13.5% more GMS in the following year. The strongest lifts came from Lapsed and Zero Time buyers. 60% of Lapsed and 50% of Zero Time redeemers stayed active a year later, versus 50% and 29% for non-redeemers.

The redirect tradeoff showed in the data. First-party digital purchases declined an average of 20% year over year, the cost of sending buyers off-site.

Reflection

The redirect was the right call to launch the program in 2024. The +30% value purchased and the new physical and third-party channels are gains we would not have had with a fully in-house program. But the -20% on first-party digital is now visible enough that the next phase of work is exploring bringing first-party digital back in-house while TDS continues to run physical and third-party.

The bigger lesson: when the strategic gain is large enough, accepting constraints on one surface can be the right tradeoff. The design job is to make sure the constraint is tracked, named, and revisited.
