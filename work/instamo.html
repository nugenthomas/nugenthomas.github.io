Instamo

Product Design
2020

When I joined Instamo, the beta money-transfer app had usability friction that eroded user trust as they sent large sums abroad. I led the design revamp for alpha launch across onboarding, transactions, and tracking. After alpha launched, new users grew 298% in 28 days.

Trust was the design problem

Instamo's beta money-transfer app served Southeast Asians abroad sending money home to family. But unclear forms and confusing tracking made users question whether to trust the app with their money. Once money crosses a border, there's no pulling it back, so every step in the flow had to feel safe.

I joined to lead the design revamp for alpha launch alongside Instamo's in-house team. The constraint: rework the experience without rewriting the back-end.

Cutting onboarding friction

The legacy onboarding had too many screens and redundant fields. Worse, 21.4% of beta users bounced on the ID upload step. 32% of our base relied on others to handle English paperwork in daily life, so complex forms hit them hardest. My hypothesis: they didn't trust the app with their legal identity yet.

I split the form into Sign-up and Identity Verification. The team also made two trust-building decisions: integrating Onfido for faster, more secure KYC, and keeping a manual entry fallback for users who didn't want to use their camera, reviewed within 48 hours.

Streamlining the send money flow

Sending money internationally requires a lot of information. The legacy flow surfaced it all at once and overwhelmed users.

I chunked the form into purposeful steps, surfacing only what was needed at each, with a clear path forward.

After confirming the transaction, users still had to manually transfer from their bank to Instamo, a backend constraint we couldn't change. I designed clear payment instructions, a receipt upload step, and status updates that closed the loop on the transfer.

Making "where is my money?" answerable

The legacy tracking screen used back-end status codes that didn't map to anything in the user's head. "Verify transaction" and "create smart contract" meant nothing to a foreign worker checking on their money. Users were left guessing.

I rebuilt the tracker around three states (Awaiting payment, Paid, Needs attention) and first-person plain language. "You set up your transfer. You sent your USD to Instamo. We received your USD. We are sending out your VND." Each state mapped to a specific next action.

Outcome

After the alpha launch, new users grew 298% in the following 28 days, with 7-day new users up 321% and 1-day up 50%. Completed transactions reached 340 in the first four months. The redesign validated the hypothesis that reducing form friction and clarifying tracking would translate directly into more completed sign-ups.
