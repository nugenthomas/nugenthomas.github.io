Etsy: Refund to Credit

Product Design
2024

In 2023, Etsy processed 13.7M refunds totaling $501M. A case system MVP validated that buyers would opt for Etsy Credit instead. I led design across the expansion: seller-initiated refunds, Member Services tooling, an incentive experiment, and UK/EU launch. Today, ~20% of refunds opt into RTC, buyers overspend the credit by ~95%, and RTC contributes ~0.5% of total Etsy GMS.

Refunds were a hidden growth lever

In 2023, Etsy processed 13.7 million refunds totaling $501M. Every refund took 3 to 5 business days for buyers to get their money back, and the funds left the Etsy ecosystem entirely. The wait hurt time-sensitive shoppers like gift buyers, and the dollars left the platform with no reason to come back.

A case system MVP earlier in 2024, owned by the Buyer Support and Payments Platform squads, validated the bet. 22% of eligible buyers opted into credit, and their 30-day repurchase rate jumped from 46% to 72%. The mechanic was real. The next step was expanding it across every refund path on Etsy.

Encourage credit. Never mislead.

The buyer chooses the refund method. Credit is presented clearly alongside the original payment method. Each option states what it gives the buyer: instant access and no expiration for credit, the familiar 3 to 5 day return for original payment.

For seller-initiated refunds, the buyer has 48 hours to choose. If they do not, the refund defaults to the original payment method. Buyers should never feel tricked into a credit they did not want.

Designing the full flow

The expansion covered four workstreams: seller-initiated refunds (when sellers cancel or approve a refund request), Member Services-initiated refunds (when agents issue refunds via Atlas), an incentive experiment testing a 5% or 10% bonus credit on top of the refund, and a UK/EU launch with regulatory complexity, especially Germany.

Across these flows, I designed the buyer choice surface, the status updates that follow on Convos, the Purchases page, and the receipt, the Member Services tooling in Atlas, and the email touchpoints that confirm each refund.

I partnered closely with Risk to design the fraud guardrails. Frozen and banned sellers cannot issue Refund to Credit. High-risk sellers can be paused while their accounts are reviewed. Credit balances are capped per locale, and Risk runbooks let the team reverse credits en masse when collusion is detected.

Other functions shaped the work alongside me. Legal partnership defined UK and EU eligibility, including Germany, where the team layered IP, billing address, and locale signals to limit exposure. Accounting partnership defined entity tagging so each refund credits the right book of business. Member Services partnership shaped the Atlas tooling so agents could issue, view, and reverse credits inline.

Outcome

At scale, ~20% of refunds opt into RTC. Most buyers spend the full credit within 30 days. The strongest behavioral signal is overspend: buyers redeeming refund credits spend ~95% above the credit value on average. They don't just spend the refund, they spend nearly double.

Today, RTC contributes ~0.5% of total Etsy GMS. The expansion projected $9.9M in annualized recovered revenue with a 13% lift in post-refund repurchase rate, and $118K in payment processing savings.

Reflection

The MVP tested two messaging variants and they performed identically. Whether the copy emphasized "instant access" or "shop again right away," buyers responded the same. The mechanic was the value, not the framing. Later, the Incentive experiment tested whether a bonus credit on top of the refund would push opt-in higher. It did lift opt-in and overspend, but not enough to drive additional purchases post-redemption. RTC's intrinsic mechanic was already doing the work the bonus aimed to add.
