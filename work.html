Etsy: Purchase Reward

Product Design
2025

Etsy had run discount-based offers for years, but had not tested whether buyers would respond to earning credit. I designed Etsy's first credit-based purchase reward across web, mweb, and the native app. The $5 for $25+ variant ramped up for new and zero-time buyers, projected to drive $20.5M in annualized incremental GMS.

Etsy knew discounts worked. Credit was the open question.

Etsy had been running discount-based bounce-back offers for years, and they worked. Credit was a different lever. The Stored Value squad needed to know if buyers would respond to it the way they respond to a coupon.

A discount is immediate value. A credit is delayed value: earn it on this purchase, spend it on the next. The hypothesis was that credit would create a different motion. Stretch to qualify now, come back to redeem later. If it worked, it would set the foundation for Etsy's loyalty work.

Earn now, spend later

The bet was that a small, automatic credit reward could lift two things: the size of the qualifying purchase, and the likelihood of a repeat one. The cost to Etsy lands after the conversion is locked in, which made the math attractive if buyers actually behaved that way.

Threshold nudges across the journey

I designed threshold nudges across the buyer's journey on web, mweb, and the native app.

A skinny banner and a targeted prompt surface the offer first. As buyers shop, a progress bar tracks them toward qualifying, in the right column on web and as a bottom sheet on mobile. Visible enough to motivate, light enough to not crowd the path to checkout.

I made the credit automatic. No code, no redemption step. It lands in the Etsy Balance after payment and applies at checkout the next time the buyer visits. Fewer steps between earning and using means a higher chance of redemption.

I made sign-in a requirement for the credit, but kept guest checkout open. Guests could buy without signing in, but only signed-in buyers could keep the reward. That turned account creation into a measurable second outcome.

I designed the threshold celebration to feel earned, not loud. When the buyer crosses the spend threshold, the bar transforms with a small animation that registers the win without pulling them out of the shopping flow.

Closing the loop

Earning the credit was only half the loop. The other half was getting buyers to come back and spend it before it expired. I designed a four-stage reminder cadence across push and email, with the tone shifting to match the buyer's mental state at each stage.

Within the first week, while the buyer is still in shopping mode, the tone is invitation. By 30 to 45 days, when the credit is fading from memory, it becomes gentle reminder. In the last two weeks, the cadence turns to urgency. Each stage pairs a push notification with an email, and the curated picks shift to match the tone.

Outcome

The 90-day analysis showed the $5 for $25+ variant drove statistically significant, ROI positive lifts in purchase and engagement behaviors for new and zero-time buyers. Etsy ramped up that variant to those segments, projected to drive $20.5M in annualized incremental GMS.

Both hypotheses held. Buyers were motivated to spend to earn the credit, then came back to use it. The sign-in requirement worked too: all treatment variants showed stat sig lifts to account creations from new buyers. The work set the foundation for Etsy's ongoing credit-based offers in the loyalty portfolio.
